# Nuketown 2025 — Concept Quality Bar 2 (CONCEPT-BAR-2): Atomic Acres weak points

Target: **BO2-2025 only.** Companion to `docs/reference/CONCEPT-BAR.md`; it covers
the houses, coach, plaza, yards and street section. This file covers only what the
`docs/reference/concept2/` set was generated to answer: the turning circle at
eye level, house interiors, the east side plus third house, ground/paving
close-ups, and mannequins.

How to use this file: each subject has four lenses — **(a)** material behaviour
with roughness/value ranges, **(b)** value composition with luminance ratios,
**(c)** detail density at 40 m versus 2 m with millimetre dimensions,
**(d)** colour relationships with area percentages and never-saturate rules.
Statements are falsifiable on purpose: a builder can implement them, a critic can
check them in a capture. All claims are grounded in what the concept2 PNGs
actually show (59 files, see `docs/reference/concept2/manifest.json`).

Global notes that apply everywhere:

- Palette picks are **families, not droppers**. Judge relationships, not numbers;
  no calibrated hex is mandated anywhere below.
- SPEC OPEN items are **not mandated** anywhere below: no front-window ledge
  requirement, no mailboxes, no exact turning-head inset dimension, no calibrated
  hex.
- Light is harsh desert noon unless the file's light slot says otherwise: sun
  near-overhead, shadows short and dark, sky bounce strong. Lowsun files show the
  same materials raking and warm; overcast files show them flat and cool.

## 1. Turning circle + vehicles at eye level

Reference reads: `turn-vehicles-noon-eye-01…04`, `-low-01`, `-high34-01`,
`-plan-01`, `-lowsun-*`, `-overcast-eye-01` (10 files). The set shows a small-town
roundabout: a circular ring road of pale asphalt around a round concrete kerb
island with radial groove joints, a cream intercity coach with a maroon swoosh
standing at the kerb, and blockier truck/saloon masses opposite.

**(a) Material behaviour.** Coach enamel roughness ~0.25–0.4 with one long soft
body-side highlight; chrome belt and wheel-arch trim ~0.08–0.15 throwing hard sun
glints. The concept eye-level frames show the coach flank reading as a single
gloss band against matte asphalt (~0.9, zero specular except faint wheel-polish
sheen) — that enamel/asphalt roughness step is the whole vehicle read. Kerb
concrete ~0.85 with a darker grit wash in the bottom ~40 mm; the radial groove
joints on the island ring must catch shadow, not read as painted lines. Dirt:
tyre-polish darkening arcs where vehicles habitually stand, oil-drop dark spots
~100–200 mm under engine positions, dust drift against the kerb upstand.

**(b) Value composition.** Darkest large plane: the asphalt ring (~15–25%
lightness). Brightest: the coach cream flank in sun (~85%). The coach must pop
against the asphalt at **5:1 luminance or better** — that contrast is the read.
The kerb island reads as a pale disc (~70%) floating in the dark ring; the
eye-level concepts hold at least **3:1** between island top and adjacent asphalt
or the circle collapses into one grey plane. Coach shadow side must stay at
least **2:1** below its sun side or the vehicle reads as unlit.

**(c) Detail density at 40 m versus 2 m.** At 40 m (high34/plan): one pale kerb
circle, the coach as a long rounded slab on one side, truck + saloon as a
blockier mass opposite — three masses maximum, no street furniture competing.
At 2 m (eye/low): coach window rubber gaskets, rivet pitch ~50 mm, wheel
whitewall ring, kerb radial joints (~20–30 joints around the island, each a
recessed groove ≥10 mm deep so noon throws a line shadow), oil spots 100–200 mm
across. The coach must sit ON the asphalt with a visible contact shadow band
≤150 mm tall at the skirts — the concept lowsun-low frame shows dust and shadow
gathering under the sills, never daylight under the vehicle.

**(d) Colour relationships.** Coach: ~75% cream/silver neutral, ~15% maroon
swoosh, ~10% chrome/glass by flank area. Maroon is the only saturated colour
allowed to touch the asphalt in this area. Asphalt and kerb stay near-achromatic
(chroma ~0, warm grey only); boundary timber behind stays mid brown, never
orange. What never saturates: the road surface and the kerb — if either goes
brown or blue the turning head reads as a diorama base, not a road.

## 2. House interiors

Reference reads: `interior-living-noon-eye-01`, `-noon-low-01`,
`-lowsun-eye-01`, `-overcast-eye-01`, `interior-stair-*`, `interior-ceiling-*`,
`interior-kitchen-red-*`, `interior-kitchen-blue-*`, `interior-reveal-*`
(13 subject files + `interior-test-01`). The set shows a double-height living
room with a full-height glass wall and sheers, low credenza + sectional sofa,
globe pendant lights, terrazzo floor, a timber stair with open risers, and two
galley kitchens (saturated red cabinetry; paleblue cabinetry).

**(a) Material behaviour.** Window glass roughness ~0.08–0.15: must return sky
(pale blue-grey), never near-black at noon — the living-eye concept shows the
glass wall reading brighter than the interior, with sheer curtains diffusing it
to ~0.6. Enamel cabinetry (red kitchen) ~0.25 with a soft door-front highlight;
chrome oven/stovetop trim ~0.1 with hard glints. Timber stair and credenza
~0.7–0.8 with along-grain streak, no mirror highlight. Terrazzo floor ~0.4–0.5:
a broad soft sheen carrying the window-wall reflection, never a mirror. Globe
pendants: opal glass, emissive OFF at noon (they read white, not glowing).

**(b) Value composition.** Brightest: the window wall and sheers in sun
(~85–90%). Darkest: oven cavity, stair under-void, ceiling coffers in shade
(~10–20%). Interior mid walls sit ~55–65% so rooms read sunlit, not cave-dark.
Falsifiable budgets: window-wall to shaded side-wall span at least **4:1**;
red cabinet sun-to-shadow on the same run at least **2:1** or the kitchen reads
as flat paint; the stair void must hold at least one patch at or below 20%
lightness or the stair has no depth.

**(c) Detail density at 40 m versus 2 m.** Interiors are never a 40 m subject —
their long view is through glass from outside (a bright slot with curtain folds
and pendant globes as ticks). At 2 m (detail files): cabinet door gaps 3–5 mm
with cup-pull shadows, oven handle as a standoff bar ~25 mm off the face,
mullion rhythm on the tall narrow windows with reveals set back ≥50 mm so
reveals throw shade, stair baluster spacing with open risers showing daylight
between treads (~180 mm riser, ~250 mm going). Reveal-detail concepts show
plaster corner beads and a ≤10 mm shadow gap at junctions — flat butt joints
with no shadow read as paper, not construction.

**(d) Colour relationships.** Red kitchen: saturated red covers ~40–50% of the
cabinet wall, balanced by near-white counters and pale floor; nothing else in
the room may compete in chroma. Blue kitchen: pale blue at ~30% with white
elsewhere. Living room: ~70% warm neutrals (timber, oatmeal upholstery,
terrazzo), accent carried only by pendant cords, cushions and planting at
≤15%. What never saturates: ceilings and interior stucco (keep chroma near
zero), and the two kitchens never share a room — red tops face the orange
house side, blue tops the white house side, never mixed.

## 3. East side + third house

Reference reads: `east-sideyard-*`, `east-gable-*`, `east-passage-*`,
`east-fence-*`, `east-eave-*`, `east-roofline-*` and `third-vista-*`,
`third-massing-*`, `third-street-*`, `third-approach-*` (10 subject files +
`east-test-01`). The set shows a long low Googie side elevation with a
saturated orange fascia band over a cream wall, cantilevered eaves, dark hedge
lines, gravel ground with scrub — and a separate long white third house with a
red triangular accent wall, ribbon windows and a needle/spire on a roof disc
closing the street vista.

**(a) Material behaviour.** Fascia enamel (orange band) ~0.3–0.4: the glossiest
exterior band on this side, carrying one continuous sun streak along its length
— the sideyard-eye concept shows it reading as a single unbroken highlight
line. Cream wall stucco ~0.5–0.65 with a soft sky sheen. Eave soffits: darkest
flat surfaces in daylight, matte ~0.8, reading near-black in the passage-hero
frame. Hedge foliage ~0.9, no specular ever. Gravel ground ~0.95 with stone
glints only as points. Third-house ribbon glass ~0.1–0.2 returning sky; red
accent wall matte ~0.7 like the orange house terracotta.

**(b) Value composition.** Brightest: cream walls and paving slabs in sun
(~80–90%). Darkest: hedge cores, passage shadow slot, ribbon-window interiors
(~10–20%). The orange fascia sits mid (~45–55%) and must hold at least **2:1**
sun-to-shadow on its own face or the long elevation reads flat. The passage
between houses must read as a genuine shade slot: passage walls at least
**3:1** below sunlit walls. Third-house white sits ~85%+ against desert ground
~50–60% — the long white bar must separate from sky (~75–80%) by value edge,
not by outline.

**(c) Detail density at 40 m versus 2 m.** At 40 m (vista/street): the east read
is one long horizontal speed-line — unbroken fascia band, cantilevered eave
shadow slot, hedge base line; the third house closes the vista as a white bar
with one red triangle and one vertical needle. No vertical element may break
the fascia band. At 2 m (fence/eave/roofline): fence board gaps with
see-through slits (~8–12 mm shadow lines), eave edge thickness ≤150 mm reading
as a thin slab, fascia-to-wall shadow gap ≥15 mm, roof-drip bead. The needle
must be genuinely slender (≤300 mm at base) against the sky — a fat pylon
merges with the roof disc and the vista fails.

**(d) Colour relationships.** East elevation: ~60% cream/white neutral, ~15–20%
orange fascia accent, ~15% dark hedge green, remainder glass/sky. Third house:
~70% white, ~15% red accent wall, ~10% glass, needle white. Accent-to-neutral
on either elevation stays ≤25%. What never saturates: the gravel ground (pale
warm grey-pink, chroma low), the fence timber (tan/brown, never orange stain),
and the hedges (dark dusty green, never emerald) — saturated planting would
fight the fascia for the eye.

## 4. Ground / paving close-up

Reference reads: `ground-asphalt-*`, `ground-kerb-*`, `ground-lawnedge-*`,
`ground-patio-*`, `ground-paving-*`, `ground-sandspill-*`, `ground-stepping-*`
(12 subject files + `ground-test-01`). The set shows bleached concrete/asphalt
planes with joints and cracks, round grooved kerb-island rings, lawn-to-slab
edging, moss-darkened patio joints, sand spilling past a pit frame, and
stepping stones sitting in lawn.

**(a) Material behaviour.** Paving slabs: honed concrete ~0.6–0.7 with a faint
broom streak; joints recessed ~10 mm and grit-dark. Asphalt: ~0.9, zero
specular except faint wheel-polish sheen in the tyre arcs. Kerb tops edge-worn
paler for ~15 mm where feet and tyres polish them; kerb faces carry the
bottom-40-mm grit wash. Lawn: matte ~0.9, no specular ever. Sand ~0.95, the
brightest matte on the ground. Moss in patio joints: matte, darker and greener
than any adjacent material. Dirt is localised: clippings on the first slab row,
rust weep ≤20 mm under edging-post bases, never a global beige overlay.

**(b) Value composition.** Road/asphalt is the dark datum (~15–20%); pavements
~70–75%; lawns ~40–50% stepping between. Falsifiable: sunlit slab to its own
joint shadow at least **2:1** or joints are painted lines; kerb top to kerb
face at least **1.5:1** (three faces must read: worn-pale top, mid riser,
grit-dark base); lawn-to-slab edge must hold a dark dotted or shadow line
(~20%) against both or the edge dissolves; sand spill (~80%) against lawn
(~45%) at least **2:1**.

**(c) Detail density at 40 m versus 2 m.** At 40 m ground is only value bands —
dark road trench, pale pavement strips, lawn planes. All close-up detail lives
at 2 m and closer (macro files): slab module 600–900 mm consistent along a run
(never mixed random sizes), joint recess ~10 mm grit-filled, kerb segment
joints every ~900 mm with ~5 mm recess, lawn edge steel/timber strip ≤10 mm
proud with soil mounded above the lawn, stepping stones with tops ≤20 mm proud
of the turf (sitting IN the lawn, never floating discs), sand spill ≤200 mm
past the pit frame, patio moss only inside joints. The kerb-macro concept shows
radial groove joints as genuine recesses throwing line shadows at noon.

**(d) Colour relationships.** Ground planes are ~90% neutral (grey road, pale
paving, green lawn, tan sand); joints, moss and grime are value shifts, not
hues. Accent colours never touch the ground except as spill (sand on lawn,
clippings on slabs) at ≤5% of a frame. What never saturates: paving, kerbs,
road and joint grit (all near-achromatic; warmth ≤ a hint), and lawn edges
(brown-frayed within ~100 mm of paths is value/matte change, not a colour
border).

## 5. Mannequins

Reference reads: `mannequin-family-noon-eye-01`, `-overcast-eye-01`,
`mannequin-pair-noon-low-01`, `mannequin-bare-noon-eye-01`,
`mannequin-neck/wrist/knee/cloth-noon-macro-01`, `mannequin-group-lowsun-*`,
`mannequin-couple-lowsun-low-01` (10 subject files + `mannequin-test-01`). The
set shows articulated display figures posed as neighbours — some dressed
(magenta/purple shift dresses, red tailoring, white gloves), some bare —
with glossy fibreglass faces, painted features, and visible limb joints; macro
files isolate the neck seam, wrist joint with buckle cuff, knee seam, and cloth
weave.

**(a) Material behaviour.** Bare fibreglass: smooth matte ~0.5–0.6, one soft limb
highlight, no skin specular — the bare-eye concept shows limbs reading as
single-value tubes with a narrow sheen edge. Painted clothing panels:
cloth-rough ~0.85, no sheen at all; the family-eye concept shows magenta
dresses holding full chroma in sun with folds reading as value steps, never as
shine. The body/cloth roughness step is what reads as "dressed". Joints (neck,
wrist, knee): slightly polished ~0.4 from handling with a hairline seam shadow;
macro files show the seam as a genuine recessed line (≥1 mm) throwing shadow,
never a drawn line. White gloves ~0.7 matte. Dirt: scuff-darkened feet and
lower legs, dust on shoulders and heads, seam-line grime — never rust, never
chipped to substrate; these are maintained showroom figures.

**(b) Value composition.** Bare bodies near-white (~80–85%) — second-brightest
after sunlit walls; dressed figures carry a saturated mid (~35–50% for the
magenta/purple dresses). Faces ~75–80% so painted features (brows, lips)
separate by value as well as chroma. Falsifiable: bare limb sun-to-shadow at
least **2:1** or limbs read as glowing tubes; dress fold crest-to-trough at
least **1.5:1** or cloth reads as plastic sheet; joint seam line at least
**2:1** darker than adjacent fibreglass or the articulation is invisible past
3 m.

**(c) Detail density at 40 m versus 2 m.** At 40 m a mannequin is a two-value
tick: pale body or saturated dress column with a dark head-dot — pose
silhouette (stance, arm angle) must read before any feature. At 2 m: painted
facial features as crisp edges (brow/lip lines ≤2 mm), wig-hair strand
separation, glove cuff edge with stitching (~2 mm pitch per the wrist macro),
dress button fronts and belt buckles as standoff objects with hairline shadows.
Cloth macro shows weave and seam stitching at thread scale — buttons sit proud
≥3 mm with real shadow; printed buttons fail instantly. Fingers must be
separate digits with inter-finger shadow gaps, never a mitten mass.

**(d) Colour relationships.** Per figure: one accent maximum (magenta dress, red
suit, or bare neutral) — dress colour covers ~60–70% of a dressed figure, skin
neutral the rest. Across a group: ≤2 dress hues per frame, never adjacent
figures in clashing saturated colours. Faces stay pale neutral; lips/brows are
the only saturated points on a face at ≤2% of figure area. What never
saturates: bare fibreglass (chroma near zero — warmth comes from sun, not the
material), white gloves (stay white; if they go yellow the figure reads as
decayed, not displayed), and joint seams (value lines, never dark coloured
rings).
